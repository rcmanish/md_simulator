\begin{abstract}
This paper presents the design, implementation, and performance evaluation of a 3D Lennard-Jones Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulator in C++. Code and scripts are available at \url{https://github.com/rcmanish/md_simulator}. Starting from a verified serial baseline, we develop shared-memory (OpenMP) and distributed-memory (MPI) parallel implementations, followed by a hybrid MPI+OpenMP configuration. The model is rigorously verified for NVE energy conservation ($<$0.61\% drift, $N=256$, $\Delta t=0.005$), thermostat-driven equilibration, and timestep convergence, including stress-testing edge cases. Timer-based profiling identifies the $O(N^2)$ \texttt{compute\_forces} loop as the dominant bottleneck ($>$99.3\% of wall-clock time), motivating parallelisation. Our OpenMP implementation achieves $5.90\times$ speedup at 8 threads (73.7\% efficiency), with scaling limited by the critical-section reduction and L3 cache bandwidth. The MPI 1D spatial decomposition achieves $6.94\times$ speedup at $P=8$ ranks (86.8\% efficiency) and maintains $>$81\% weak-scaling efficiency. The hybrid $4\text{r}\times4\text{t}$ configuration yields the highest throughput ($13.2\times$ over serial), effectively balancing node-level MPI communication with shared-memory cache utilisation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem Framing}
Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations track the trajectories of interacting atoms or molecules over time by numerically integrating Newton's equations of motion. MD is a quintessential High-Performance Computing (HPC) problem because the calculation of pairwise intermolecular forces exhibits a time complexity of $O(N^2)$ in its naïve form. Even with a cutoff radius $r_c$, the workload per timestep remains immense for large systems ($N > 10^4$). This computational intensity, coupled with the need for millions of timesteps to observe macroscopic physical phenomena, necessitates massive parallelisation. 

The core challenge in parallelising MD lies in managing data locality and memory bandwidth. The continuous movement of particles dictates dynamic memory access patterns, while force reduction across multiple threads or distributed nodes introduces critical synchronisation and communication overhead. Effectively addressing these issues using shared-memory and distributed-memory architectures is essential for scalable performance.

\section{Mathematical and Algorithmic Model}
The system simulates $N$ particles in a cubic periodic box of side $L$, interacting via the Lennard-Jones (LJ) potential:
\begin{equation}
  V(r) = 4\epsilon \!\left[ \!\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12} \!-\! \left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6} \right],
  \label{eq:lj}
\end{equation}
with cutoff $r_c = 2.5\sigma$ and minimum-image periodic boundary conditions (PBC). Reduced LJ units ($\epsilon=\sigma=m=1$) are used.

Time integration uses the velocity Verlet algorithm~\cite{b7}:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{v}(t + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}) &= \mathbf{v}(t) + \tfrac{\mathbf{F}(t)}{m}\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}, \\
  \mathbf{r}(t + \Delta t)            &= \mathbf{r}(t) + \mathbf{v}(t + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2})\Delta t, \\
  \mathbf{v}(t + \Delta t)            &= \mathbf{v}(t+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}) + \tfrac{\mathbf{F}(t+\Delta t)}{m}\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}.
\end{align}
This symplectic integrator ensures $O(\Delta t^2)$ global truncation error and excellent long-time energy conservation, critical for the microcanonical (NVE) ensemble.

\section{Design Decisions}
Several architectural choices were made to optimize execution:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data Layout (SoA vs. AoS):} A Structure-of-Arrays (SoA) layout is chosen over an Array-of-Structures (AoS). SoA ensures that $x$, $y$, and $z$ coordinates are stored contiguously, maximising cache-line utilisation and enabling compiler-driven SIMD vectorisation.
    \item \textbf{OpenMP Reduction (Thread-Private vs. Atomics):} To prevent data races when accumulating $\mathbf{F}_{ij}$ and $\mathbf{F}_{ji}$ concurrently, we allocate thread-private force arrays rather than using \texttt{atomic} updates. Atomics introduce severe cache-line invalidation overhead (false sharing) in the hot loop. Thread-private arrays incur a memory overhead of $O(p \times N)$ and a final reduction cost, but vastly improve inner-loop bandwidth.
    \item \textbf{MPI Decomposition (1D vs. 3D):} A 1D spatial slab decomposition was implemented. While 3D decomposition offers an asymptotically superior surface-to-volume communication ratio ($O(N^{2/3}/P)$ vs $O(N/P)$), a 1D decomposition simplifies ghost-exchange logic and avoids corner-communication phases. For the core counts evaluated ($P \le 16$), the 1D overhead is sufficiently minimal.
\end{itemize}

Similarly, setting density too high ($\rho \ge 1.5$) caused severe initial overlaps and numerical overflow in the $r^{-12}$ repulsive term, highlighting the physical limits of the LJ potential.

\section{Parallelisation}

\subsection{OpenMP Approach}
We parallelise the outer $i$-loop of the force computation. 
\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{plots/md/omp_schedule_comparison.pdf}}
\caption{Wall time and speedup for three OpenMP schedules ($N=2000$, 8 threads).}
\label{fig:omp_sched}
\end{figure}
As seen in Fig.~\ref{fig:omp_sched}, the \texttt{dynamic} schedule outperforms \texttt{static} by 1.81$\times$. The triangular loop space assigns $N-1-i$ interactions to row $i$. A static distribution causes massive load imbalance, as early threads process far more pairs. Dynamic scheduling allocates chunks at runtime, alleviating this imbalance at the cost of minor scheduling overhead.

\subsection{MPI Approach}
For distributed memory, the 1D spatial domain decomposition partitions the box into $P$ slabs. Particles within $r_c$ of slab boundaries are passed to neighbours as \emph{ghost particles}. The volume of communication is directly proportional to the surface area of these boundary layers.

\section{Performance Analysis}

\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{plots/md/omp_speedup_efficiency.pdf}}

\subsection{OpenMP Performance}
OpenMP strong scaling (Fig.~\ref{fig:omp_speedup}) yields a $5.90\times$ speedup at 8 threads ($73.7\%$ efficiency). Fitting Amdahl's law identifies a serial fraction $f_\mathrm{serial} \approx 5.2\%$, capping the theoretical speedup at $S_\mathrm{max} \approx 19\times$. The drop in efficiency is tied to the $O(N)$ reduction phase inside the critical section and the L3 cache bandwidth exhaustion caused by concurrent writes to thread-private arrays.

\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{plots/md/mpi_strong_scaling.pdf}}

\begin{figure}[htbp]

MPI achieves $6.94\times$ speedup at $P=8$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:mpi_strong}). MPI avoids the critical-section reduction penalty present in OpenMP, allowing independent force accumulation. Weak scaling (Fig.~\ref{fig:mpi_weak}) shows 81.0\% efficiency at $P=8$. Gustafson's Law predicts near-ideal weak scaling, but the 1D slab decomposition causes the ghost-exchange surface-to-volume ratio to grow with $P$, slightly degrading efficiency.

\subsection{Hybrid MPI+OpenMP}
\begin{figure}[htbp]

The $4\text{r}\times4\text{t}$ configuration achieves the highest throughput ($13.2\times$, Fig.~\ref{fig:hybrid}). By assigning 4 threads per MPI rank, the hybrid approach reduces the number of ghost-exchange messages by $4\times$ compared to pure MPI, while perfectly saturating the L3 cache of a single NUMA domain without incurring cross-socket memory penalties.

\section{Discussion}
The performance landscape of our MD simulator is fundamentally bound by memory architecture. The LJ kernel possesses an arithmetic intensity of $\approx 0.83$ FLOP/byte, placing it firmly in the memory-bound regime of the Roofline model. As thread counts increase, contention for shared memory bandwidth throttles execution, establishing the $S_\mathrm{max}$ limit observed in our Amdahl analysis. Furthermore, the selection of SoA layout and thread-private buffers prevents catastrophic false-sharing, demonstrating that data alignment is as critical as algorithm selection in HPC.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
This work successfully developed a high-performance 3D MD simulator incorporating rigorous mathematical verification, performance profiling, and optimal parallel execution strategies across OpenMP and MPI. The $4\text{r}\times4\text{t}$ hybrid configuration provided the optimal $13.2\times$ speedup.

\textbf{Future Work:} To address the asymptotic $O(N^2)$ bottleneck, the implementation of a Cell-Linked List (Verlet neighbour list) is critical. By partitioning the simulation domain into cells of size $r_c \times r_c \times r_c$, force evaluations can be restricted to particles within adjacent cells. Since particle density is constant, the number of neighbours per particle becomes bounded, reducing the force calculation complexity from $O(N^2)$ to $O(N)$. At $N=5000$, this promises an estimated $100\times$ execution time reduction.
